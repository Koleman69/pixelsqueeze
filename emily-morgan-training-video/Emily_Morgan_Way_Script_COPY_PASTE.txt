----------------------------------------------------------------------

[KOLE]
A guest decides how they feel about a hotel in the first few seconds. Before they see the room. Before they taste a drink at Oro. It's the greeting.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
SCENE: The three moves
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Setting: Animated graphic.
Visuals: GFX greeting_three_moves

[KOLE]
Three moves. One: notice the guest. Pause a non-essential task, when it's safe to do so. Two: face them, with open posture and natural eye contact. Three: give a friendly greeting and a clear next step.

[KOLE]
Already helping someone? Acknowledge the next guest and let them know you'll be right with them. And never stop a safety-critical task to say hi.

[KOLE]
Use a guest's name when you know it and it's appropriate, but don't overdo it. And never, ever announce a room number or private information out loud.

SCENE: Language basics
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Setting: Kole avatar.
Visuals: GFX ch04_card

[KOLE]
Words matter. The same answer can make a guest feel helped or brushed off, depending on how you say it.

Clear, respectful language. Calm volume. No profanity, no sarcasm, no confusing slang, no pet names like "honey" or "buddy," and no internal hotel jargon. Guests don't know what "OOO" or "the MOD" means. And listen all the way through before you answer.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
SCENE: Cues are clues, not proof
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Setting: Kole avatar.
Visuals: GFX ch05_card | GFX notice_ask_offer_confirm

[KOLE]
Great hospitality professionals are great observers. But here's the important part: body language gives you clues, not proof.

[KOLE]
A guest looking around might need directions. Short answers might mean they want a quick interaction. But folded arms, silence, limited eye contact, or an accent? Those tell you nothing about someone's mood or ability. Don't assume. Ask a respectful question and let the guest tell you what they need.

[KOLE]
Here's the sequence. Notice a cue. Ask a useful question. Offer a relevant action. Confirm it helped. And if the guest says no thanks, respect that "no."

[KOLE]
One firm rule: never touch a guest, their belongings, or a mobility device without permission.

CHAPTER 7: WHEN THINGS GO WRONG  -  Problem solving with ownership
Time 33:00 to 41:53  (8.9 min)

----------------------------------------------------------------------
SCENE: The 5 Steps of Ownership
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Setting: Kole avatar with animated stair-step graphic.
Visuals: GFX ch07_card | GFX five_steps

[KOLE]
Things will go wrong. Rooms get cold. Cars get delayed. Requests get missed. What guests remember most is not the problem. It's how we handled it.

[KOLE]
Five steps. One: Listen. Let the guest finish, and understand how it affected them. Two: Acknowledge and apologize. "I'm sorry your room is uncomfortable." Three: Act. Ask what would help, explain realistic options, and work within your authority.

[KOLE]
Four: Hand off clearly. Give the next person the issue and the agreed action, so the guest never has to start over. Five: Follow through. Record the request, agree on a specific update time, and check that the solution actually worked.

[KOLE]
Now listen closely. An update time is a promise to communicate. It is not a promise that the repair will be finished. If it's not fixed when the time comes, you update the guest anyway, and you escalate.

[KOLE]
And if there's immediate danger, an injury, a threat, or a security concern, follow emergency procedures and get help right away. You never have to tolerate abuse or handle a threat alone.

ON SCREEN: On-screen policy box (FILL IN): Manager on duty contact: [CONFIRM]. Request tracking tool and escalation: [CONFIRM]. Employee remedy authority/limits: [CONFIRM].
